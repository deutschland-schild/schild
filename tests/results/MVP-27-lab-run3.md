## What this is and what it is not

The same lab as before: nineteen Linux VMs on one lab host (10 cores and 24 GB of memory; each VM its own kernel, address and packet filter): sixteen gateways, one balancer VM with HAProxy 3.0 in TCP mode, one application VM, one client VM. Visitors come from 203.0.113.0/24 with real packets. Groups are set up by configuration, with the guide's one `schild.yaml` and `name_from_host`. Its limits are those of the earlier runs: not the testlab of the spec, one host and one virtual switch, blocks set by hand. The verdict in the JSON cannot be `pass`; it is `fail` because of the checks below. What a hit kill criterion means is for people to decide.

The lab host is a small machine and was not to be saturated (a note of the maintainer during the run). The throughput check is therefore three steps of 40 s up to 2 000 requests a second and has no step "as fast as it goes"; no check of this report failed while the host or the load client was saturated (load average of the host during the sixteen-node checks: 6.6 of 10 cores; during the ten minutes of load on sixteen gateways of scenario 5: 13 to 16, and every check of that scenario passed).

## How it was run

Not in one `run.sh` call. The harness got two switches for a run in parts (`KEEP=1` keeps the VMs, `REUSE=1` runs further scenarios on them without building again) and was called seven times on the same nineteen VMs and the same image:

| Call | Scenarios | What for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `s1 s2 s3 s4` | the earlier scenarios as they were (build, image, VMs) |
| 2 | `s7 s8 s9 s10 s11 s5` | the earlier scenarios as they were |
| 3 | `s12 s13 s14 s17 s20 s21` | new |
| 4 | `s18 s12 s13 s20 s19` | new; `s12`, `s13`, `s20` a second time after a correction of the scenario (below) |
| 5 | `s16 s15` | new |
| 6 | `s23 s22 s24 s25` | new; `s23` is the upgrade that `s16` could not run (below) |
| 7 | cleanup | `host.sh down` |

`run.sh` without arguments runs all of it in one call (about six hours); that was not done. Between the calls scratch scripts tried new scenarios out on three VMs that the running scenario did not use; nothing of those trials is in the results, and where one is quoted it is called a trial.

## Failures of this run

### S1. Under a steady stream of changes `shared_state` reads `differs`, and the nodes log repairs that take nothing (FAIL, 15.02, 15.22, 24.01)

Contract section 14: "a difference younger than 10 s is a change on its way, from 10 s on `GroupMember.shared_state` says `differs`, and from 30 s on the overview carries `cluster_state_differs` and the node repairs".

The stream: one change about every 0.6 s, on the nodes in turn, in all four classes: a block and its lift on another node half a second later, an allowlist entry and its removal on another node, `retention.events_days`, a login and its logout on another node. 497 changes in 300 s, no call failed. ### (d) The state comparison (scenarios 15, 24, 25)

- **Idle: always `same`**, on three nodes and on sixteen, five minutes each, asked on every node once a second (15.01, 15.21).
- **Under changes it flickers:** S1.
- **A real difference is shown.** A pair, n1 90 s ahead, a block made on n1, which n2 puts off by the 60 s rule (15.13): both nodes show `differs` in `blocks` after 11.3 s, the overview of n1 carries `cluster_state_differs` after 30.7 s, both nodes stay ready, and when the record was taken and the clock right both read `same`. In the trial before the run: 11.3 s and 15.4 s, 30.7 s.
- **A paused node is not a way to see a difference (15.10).** With n3 paused and a block, an allowlist entry and a setting made on n1, n1 shows n3 as `unreachable` and its `shared_state` as `unknown` in all four classes; n3 shows n1 as `no_contact` and `unknown`. No `differs`, no hint. After `schild cluster resume` both read `same` after 13.5 s and n3 holds the three changes (15.12).
- **What the repair does:** where the difference was a record that could not be taken yet (the clock), the hint went when the record arrived. Where the records were there and one node had not applied them (S2), the repair took no record, applied the state again and the difference was gone. Under a stream of changes (S1) it takes no record and the difference comes back with the next change.
- **With nodes of `e54c040` in the group (23.04):** a node of the current build reads a member of the earlier build as `unknown` in every class and a member of the current build as `same`; a node of the earlier build sends no field.
- **A setting that does not fit one node's `schild.yaml`** (the guide's example of a hint that stays) could not be provoked: no documented setting was found that one node's file refuses. `PATCH /settings` takes each retention value by itself.

### (e) The cheaper exchange (scenarios 16, 23)
