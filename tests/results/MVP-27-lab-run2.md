## What this is and what it is not

The same lab as in the first run: nineteen Linux VMs on one lab host (macOS on arm64, 10 cores, 24 GB, Apple `container` 1.5.0; each VM its own kernel, address and packet filter): sixteen gateways, one balancer VM with HAProxy 3.0 in TCP mode, one application VM, one client VM. Visitors come from 203.0.113.0/24 with real packets. Groups are set up by configuration. Its limits are the first run's: not the testlab of the spec, one host and one virtual switch, a Python load client, blocks set by hand. The verdict in the JSON cannot be `pass`; it is `fail` because of the checks below. What a hit kill criterion means is for people to decide.

What differs from the first run, because the documents changed:

- **One file for every node.** Every scenario but the second deploys the guide's cluster block as it is now: one `node_name` (`web`) for all nodes and `name_from_host: true`. Scenario 2 keeps a `node_name` per node and no `name_from_host` (the other way the guide names).
- **The balancer is the guide's example**, line by line, for a site without TLS (scenarios 1, 4, 5) and for a site with TLS on the gateways (7, 9). The harness adds a section `global` with the stats socket it reads the state of the servers from.
- **Kept connections come from two clients**: one that honours `Connection: close` (after such an answer it opens a new connection), and one that ignores it and sends its next request on the same socket.
- **Certificates.** A node that never was in step with its group orders nothing (contract section 5). The first run's opening step, "n1 alone obtains the certificate", is therefore no longer what the documents describe; the scenario starts the pair, and the lone node is a check of its own (7.03a).
- **A start 90 s behind** (3.50) and **a restart with no member reachable** (7.07) are judged by contract section 10 as it is now; both are named under "Passed before, differs now".

## Failures of this run

### N1. A block lifted on another node within a second stays in every block list, cannot be lifted again, and is dropped by an edge (FAIL, 11.01, 11.02, 11.04; 8 of 8 rounds)

Three nodes, 200 addresses per round. Each address is blocked on one node (`POST /api/v0/blocks`, 7 days) and lifted on another node 0.2 to 1.0 s after the block call returned (`POST /api/v0/incidents/<incident>/unblock`, with the incident number of the block's answer; a first answer 404 is sent again every 50 ms until 200). Five seconds after the last lift every node is asked for its block list and for a request of every address.

| Run | Round 1 | Round 2 | Round 3 (lift 2 to 3 s later, 100 addresses) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| first try of the scenario | 19 of 200 left | 18 of 200 | not run |
| run 2 | 16 of 200 | 13 of 200 | 0 of 100 |
| run 3 | 11 of 200 | 16 of 200 | 0 of 100 |
| run 4 (in the JSON) | 7 of 200 | 16 of 200 | 0 of 100 |

116 of 1 600 blocks (7.3 %) were left; no round was clean. The loopback run saw this once in two runs. What "left" is, from run 4 (runs 3 and 4 recorded it this way):

- **Listed everywhere, refused nowhere.** Each of the 23 addresses left in run 4 is in `GET /api/v0/blocks` of all three nodes (7 days, `node` the setting node) and counted in `schild cluster status` ("Blocks of the group that run: 24", one of them a real one). A request of the address is answered 200 on all three nodes. So the lift acted in the request path of the gateways, and the list says otherwise.
- **It does not pass.** All were still listed 30 s later, and after a restart of all three gateways (11.05: 23 listed on all three, 0 refused). In the first try the 19 of round 1 were still counted when round 2 ended, about three minutes later.
- **A person cannot lift it.** The lifting node holds the incident as `unblocked`. A second "Freigeben" there answers `409 conflict`, "Dieser Vorfall ist schon freigeschaltet." (6 of 6 tried per round), and the address stays listed.
- **An edge drops it (11.04).** A balancer VM that joined as an edge after the rounds held 23 of the 500 lifted addresses in its nftables set, each with its 7 days (`198.51.100.24 timeout 6d23h50m3s`), exactly the 23 the gateways still listed. One of these addresses was given to the client VM as a real visitor: through the balancer VM it was answered 200 before the edge ran and got no answer with the edge running. For that visitor the block runs seven more days at the edge although "Freigeben" was answered 200.
- **Which ones.** The lift of 20 of the 23 was answered 200 at once; 3 were answered 404 first (the lifting node did not hold the block yet) and 200 on a later try. The pause of the left ones spreads over the whole range (0.20 to 0.93 s). Every pair of nodes is hit in one run or another (run 4, round 2: n1>n2 2 of 34, n1>n3 5 of 33, n2>n1 3 of 33, n2>n3 1 of 34, n3>n1 2 of 33, n3>n2 3 of 33). The clocks of the three VMs were within 5 ms of the client's. With the lift 2 to 3 s after the block, 0 of 300 were left in three runs.
- Scenario 1 lifts on the other node at the moment that node lists the block (1.12b) and checks the answer only, and its timed lifts (1.12) come 1.5 to 2.2 s after the block. Neither sees this.

Evidence of every left block of run 4 (which node set, which lifted, pause, both answers, the rows of `GET /blocks` of each node, `schild cluster status` of each node, the second lift, and the timings of all 400 pairs) is in [MVP-27-lab-run2-s11-evidence.json](MVP-27-lab-run2-s11-evidence.json); the scenario writes it as `s11-round<N>-evidence.json` into the result directory of a run. ### N2. A killed node is not named as partial in most answers for the group (FAIL, 10.20, 10.20b; in 3 of 3 runs)

Three nodes, 20 requests a second on n1, n2 killed, n3 without traffic. From 75 s after the kill on, n1 and n3 were asked every 15 s for four minutes (12 answers each). Contract section 9a: `partial_nodes` is "a node whose newest bucket ends more than 45 s before the span does, with `until`".

- **`queryMetricSeries`, 10 s ring, the last two minutes (10.20):** n2 was under `cluster.partial_nodes` in 3 of 12 answers, on n1 and on n3 alike: the ones at 105, 165 and 225 s after the kill, once a minute. The nine others (75, 90, 120, 135, 150, 180, 195, 210, 240 s) carry `partial_nodes: []` and list three nodes. The single points of these answers are marked `partial: true` (12 of 12 in the last answer).
- **`getMonitoringNow` (10.20b):** 0 of 12 answers name n2. Each says it covers three nodes, none partial, and gives 20 requests a second for the group.
- **1 min ring and `getTrafficBreakdown` (10.20c, PASS):** both name n2, four minutes after the kill, on n1 and n3. The time named is `until: 15:12:00Z`; n2 was killed at 15:12:19Z. In a probe before the run the time named was 56 s before the kill.

The numbers themselves are right: every 10 s bucket of n1 across the kill holds what the client sent (10.24), and the history of n2 is still answered from n1 and n3 (10.21: 904 of 904). What is missing is the sign that the sum lacks a node. Reproduce: `run.sh s10`.

### N3. A node that is not ready refuses connections on its web ports; it does not answer 503, and its log does not say why (FAIL, 8.01b)

Contract section 10: `/.schild/readyz` "answers 503 on a node of a group until the node took the records of the group from a member"; "A node that never was ... answers 503 until it reaches a member." Guide: "bleibt auf 503".

- A fresh node whose two peers are not started (8.01, 8.01b): 148 of 148 readiness requests in 75 s ended in `Connection refused`. Port 80 is not open. `GET /api/v0/group` on it says `syncing`. A grep of its log for a line about waiting, peers or readiness finds none.
- A node that was in step once and restarts alone (8.03, three runs): about 180 refused requests, no 503, ready after 10.19, 10.17 and 10.14 s, with the block from before refused at that moment.
- A node that starts 90 s behind (3.50): 279 refused, one 503, then 200 after 29.5 s.
- With a TLS site (7.03a, 7.07): ports 80 and 443 closed in that time.

For a balancer a refused connection and a 503 are the same: the node is out. So the task's first new check holds in substance (out of traffic for at least 60 s: 8.01 PASS; after about 10 s for a node that was in step: 8.03 PASS). The documents say 503, and a person who looks at such a server finds closed ports and nothing in the log. A node that returns while a member is reachable does answer 503 first (1.26: 9 polls 503, then 200 after 1.42 s).

### N4. After one node was 90 s ahead, a node only 30 s behind takes nothing from the others (FAIL, 3.61; in 2 of 3 runs on this build, passed in the first run)

Same processes throughout: n3 90 s ahead, set right, then 30 s behind. Blocks set on n1 then did not act on n3 (three of three served after 6 s), and a login made on n1 was not valid on n3. The log of n3: `cluster: a record is put off, its time is ahead of this node's clock by more than a minute; set the clocks of the nodes right (NTP)`, `"from":"sg27-n1","ahead":"1m27s"`. n3 was 30.0 s behind n1 at that moment (the harness reads both clocks).

So the records n1 wrote carried a time about 57 s ahead of n1's own clock. Black box, I can only name what fits: the contract's record time is a hybrid logical clock (section 2), n1 had taken the records n3 wrote while it was 90 s ahead (each as soon as it was 60 s ahead, 3.21b), and its own records afterwards carry a time after those. That uses up the 60 s the rule allows. The as-written run and the first run, which spend a few seconds less between the two steps, passed (blocks on n3 within 204 ms). At 30 s on a fresh group nothing is put off (3.11, 3.12, 3.31, 3.32).

The three other failed checks of scenario 3 (3.23, 3.43, 3.62) are in the table of the 19. Whether a running node that is 90 s off is "shown as fine", as the task asks: yes, apart from the clock hints. With n3 90 s behind and taking nothing, `/.schild/readyz` of n3 answers 200, both dashboards say `active` with every member `ok`, and n1 shows the service of n3 as `ready` (3.49c; the same at 90 s ahead, 3.29c). The hints are all there: `clock_skew_ms` -89 999 on n1 and +89 996 on n3 (3.48), the reason `cluster_clock_skew` in the overview of both (3.49), the `Clock:` line of `schild cluster status` on both (3.46), and the log line of the node that puts off (3.29b, 3.49b). The thresholds are as written: at 3 s no line and no reason, at 7 s the line and no reason, at 12 s both (3.71 to 3.73).

- **The node that orders a certificate places two orders in five of eight first issuances.** 7.03: 2 orders, 1 issued. 7.08: 2, 2, 2. 7.10: 1, 1, 2. 9.01: 1. Always one node, always one certificate. With the guide's balancer for a TLS site no node is up on port 443 before the first certificate exists, so an order that the CA validates over TLS-ALPN-01 fails and the node orders again over HTTP-01. Guide: "Eine Bestellung pro Zertifikat, solange sich die Server erreichen." Counted in orders at the CA that is not so; counted in nodes that order and certificates issued it is.
- **`option redispatch` (1.21r),** which the guide names as not measured: one node of the pair killed under 200 new connections a second: 7 of 2 793 requests failed, the last 0.03 s after the kill. Without it: 34 of 1 932, the last after 3.42 s (1.21; first run 30 of 1 899).

- **The guide's `cluster:` block needs a `node_name` next to it (1.01):** without one `schild ingress validate` exits 1, `missing property 'node_name'`. The guide's sentence on `name_from_host` implies the key is there.

- **The `until` of a partial node is the start of a minute** (N2): 19 s and, in a probe, 56 s before the node was killed.

That probe kept no client-side counts, so it is not known whether the client paused or the bucket is wrong.
